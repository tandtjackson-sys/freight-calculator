Frequently Asked Questions About Freight DIM Weight

What is Dimensional Weight (DIM Weight)?

Dimensional weight, or volumetric weight, is a billing technique used by commercial freight carriers (including FedEx, UPS, DHL, and USPS) to reflect the space a package occupies in a vehicle relative to its actual gross weight. Carriers charge based on whichever value is greater: the actual scale weight or the calculated dimensional weight.

How is Dimensional Weight calculated?

Dimensional weight is calculated by multiplying the length, width, and height of a package (in inches) to determine its total cubic volume, then dividing that figure by a specific standard divisor known as the DIM Factor. The standard DIM factor for domestic US shipments is typically 139 for express air freight and parcel carriers, though exact factors vary by carrier and contract terms.

What is Freight Density and Freight Class?

Freight density measures the weight per cubic foot of a shipment. In Less-Than-Truckload (LTL) shipping, the National Motor Freight Traffic Association (NMFTA) assigns shipments a freight class ranging from Class 50 (clean, dense cargo like steel) to Class 500 (light, bulky items like ping-pong balls). Lower freight classes generally cost less to ship per pound.

How can shippers reduce DIM weight charges?

Shippers can minimize dimensional charges by using custom-sized corrugated boxes, trimming excess packaging material, nesting items efficiently, or switching from rigid boxes to poly mailers where appropriate. Accurately measuring dimensions before tendering shipments prevents costly carrier adjustment fees.
