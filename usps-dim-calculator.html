Understanding USPS Dimensional Weight Rules

The United States Postal Service applies dimensional (DIM) weight rules to domestic and international packages that exceed specific size thresholds. Unlike commercial parcel carriers like UPS or FedEx that use a 139 divisor, USPS uses a **166 DIM factor**, making dimensional weight charges slightly more favorable for shippers.

The 1 Cubic Foot Threshold Rule

USPS only calculates dimensional weight for packages that exceed **1 cubic foot (1,728 cubic inches)**. If your total cubic volume is 1,728 cubic inches or less, USPS bills strictly by actual physical weight regardless of the box size.

USPS Imperial DIM Formula

For packages greater than 1,728 cubic inches, multiply Length × Width × Height and divide by 166:

(Length × Width × Height) ÷ 166 = Dimensional Weight (lbs)

Non-Standard Length & Dimension Surcharges

In addition to DIM weight, USPS charges non-standard length fees for large boxes: packages exceeding 22 inches in length incur a length surcharge, and packages exceeding 30 inches or 2 cubic feet (3,456 cu in) are subject to additional handling fees.
